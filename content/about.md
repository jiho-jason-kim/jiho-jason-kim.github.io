## Professional biography

I am Jiho Kim, a PhD student in Computer Sciences at the University of Wisconsin–Madison. I study data visualization and human-computer interaction, focusing on visual perception, user modeling, accessibility, and AI-assisted visualization design.

I have worked as a graduate researcher at UW–Madison since September 2021. My research spans visualization under divided attention, multi-chart comparison, and accessible ways to explain and explore charts.

I earned an MS in Computer Sciences from UW–Madison in May 2025. I earned a BS in Computer Science from the University of Southern California in May 2020. My anticipated PhD completion date is May 2027.

During my undergraduate studies, I conducted research at USC's Information Sciences Institute. I supported network-traffic simulation and analysis for a framework that distinguishes DDoS attacks from flash crowds.

## Research motivation

Visualization design involves the people who interpret data as well as the data itself. My work examines attention, expectations, tasks, and access needs.

I aim to make visualizations easier to understand across these conditions. This includes studying cognitive processes and designing interactions for blind and low vision users.

## Teaching and technical experience

At UW–Madison, I have taught CS240: Introduction to Discrete Mathematics and CS839: Modeling User Interaction as a teaching assistant.

My software experience includes visualization research tools, BART-based question answering, and web applications. I have worked with Vue.js, Firebase, PHP, JavaScript, MySQL, and Linux.

## Career interests

I am interested in academic and industry research opportunities in visualization, human-computer interaction, accessibility, and AI-assisted design.

## Additional experience

From September 2015 to June 2017, I served as a Republic of Korea Army interpreter. I coordinated Korean–English translation between Korean and US military units.
