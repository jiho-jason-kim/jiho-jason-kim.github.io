## Research interests

My research connects data visualization, visual perception, and human-computer interaction. I examine how people interpret charts and how visualization design can support different users.

- **Visual perception and attention:** How do concurrent tasks affect visualization interpretation?
- **User modeling:** How can models of cognitive processes explain interpretation strategies and performance?
- **Accessibility:** How can blind and low vision users understand chart structures and explore data?
- **AI-assisted visualization design:** How do language models reason about visualization choices?

## Doctoral research

I study visualization design in relation to human perception, attention, and interaction. My PhD in Computer Sciences at the University of Wisconsin–Madison has an anticipated completion date of May 2027.

### For a general audience

People use charts while managing other tasks, comparing information, or accessing content through assistive technology. I study these situations to understand what helps people interpret data.

### For a technical audience

I design controlled experiments on visualization perception, mapping, and comprehension. I use the Linear Ballistic Accumulator framework to model cognitive processes. My work also includes eye-tracking study design, accessible chart interaction, and perceptual optimization through machine teaching.

## Research projects

### Visualization under divided attention

I designed experiments on visualization interpretation under divided attention and modeled cognitive processes. The work identifies greater reliance on designs that align with viewer expectations.

[Read the project]({{< relref "/projects/divided-attention" >}}) and [the forthcoming IEEE VIS 2026 paper]({{< relref "/publications/divided-attention" >}}).

### Multi-chart comparison

I characterize chart comparison through the joint effects of layout, mark, and task. This framework predicts viewer performance across chart arrangements.

[Explore comparison axis alignment]({{< relref "/projects/multi-chart-comparison" >}}).

### Accessible visualization

I develop methods for explaining unfamiliar charts, supporting chart questions, and selecting line-chart samples for blind and low vision users.

- [Text explanations of unfamiliar charts]({{< relref "/projects/chart-explanations" >}}).
- [Accessible chart question answering]({{< relref "/projects/accessible-chart-qa" >}}).
- [Machine teaching for accessible line-chart sampling]({{< relref "/projects/accessible-sampling" >}}).

### User modeling and AI-assisted design

I design eye-tracking studies of visualization interpretation. I also evaluate language models' reasoning about visualization design and identify recurring design strategies.

[Eye tracking and user modeling]({{< relref "/projects/eye-tracking" >}}) · [LLM visualization design]({{< relref "/projects/llm-visualization-design" >}}).

## Methods and tools

- **Experiments:** Controlled studies of perception, mapping, comprehension, and divided attention.
- **Modeling:** Linear Ballistic Accumulator models and user performance models.
- **Interaction research:** Eye-tracking study design and analysis of chart question-answering interactions.
- **Accessibility:** Learning-based chart explanations and machine teaching for perceptual optimization.
- **AI:** Language-model evaluation, automatic text generation, and BART-based question answering.
